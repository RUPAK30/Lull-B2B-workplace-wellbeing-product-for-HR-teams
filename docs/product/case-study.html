Case study: from a wellness app to a meeting's ending

How a 2½-minute desk-break app became Viraam, a close that runs at the end of a team's standup, by following the evidence and cutting what it didn't support.

Case study · Rupak Ghanpathi
Product · Business analysis

Context

Indian companies offer wellbeing benefits, but HR can't show they're used. The first version was a personal desk-break app: type how you feel, get a guided break. It competed with a free reminder inside Microsoft 365 and depended on people remembering to use it.

My role

Problem framing, research, requirements, product and safety decisions, the site and the pilot design, built and tested end to end.

What the research showed

~30%of employees take part in company wellness programmes (Plum, 3,500+ Indian orgs, 2023)
~3%of mental-health app users still active after 30 days (JMIR 2019, 93 apps)
64%of employees fear employer health data could affect pay or promotion (Aetna survey)

The failure point is starting, not content: tools that wait for people to remember get forgotten. A team already meets every day, so the meeting can be the reminder.

Key decisions, and why

DecisionRationale
Re-anchor to the last 2½ minutes of the standup, synced to the wall clockThe meeting removes the need to remember; a shared start makes it a team ritual a private reminder can't copy
One link, no accounts; HR has nothing to runEvery setup step removed raises the chance of a yes
Price per team (₹1,499/month), not per seatA team ritual priced like a team lunch, not like HR software
Only three counts per team; teams under 10 hiddenProof for HR without anyone being watched
Graded every step against evidence; cut what had noneStand and move (active beats passive); end on an if-then plan (d = .65); no productivity claim (breaks don't raise performance)
Renamed Lull → Sukoon → Viraam (विराम)पूर्ण विराम is the full stop every Hindi and Marathi reader knows; it names the ending without wellness baggage

The close

TimeStepWhy (evidence)
0:00–0:20Stand if you can; one slow breath outBreaks that add movement beat passive seated breaks (systematic review of 6 trials, 2022)
0:20–1:20Neck, shoulders, wrists, 20 s eachShort breaks raise vigor and lower fatigue (Albulescu et al., 2022, 22 studies); which moves matter is not established
1:20–1:40Look across the room, blink20-20-20 eased eye strain while practised (Aston University)
1:40–2:20"When this ends, I'll first…" (never sent)If-then plans: 94 tests, d = .65 (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006)
2:20–2:30"Back at 10:15" + "Leaving with:" card—

What I'd measure

North star: days the close ran per team per week, at week 6. Plus share of the team joining and finish rate. Not yet proven: there are no customers yet, and that number is the point of the pilot.
