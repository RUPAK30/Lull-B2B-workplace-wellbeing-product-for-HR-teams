For teams who sit all day

Three minutes between meetings. No app to learn.

Someone types “I’ve been sitting all day”. Lull suggests one short, guided stretch and stays with them through it. If it’s too easy, it adjusts. If something hurts, it stops.

How it works

  1. 1 Say what today needs

    In their own words. “Stiff back”, “ten minutes”, “low energy”. Or tap a ready-made quick break.

  2. 2 Get one short session

    A specific plan with a reason, and the real length up front. Accept it, adjust it, or pick something else.

  3. 3 It adjusts as you go

    “Too easy” makes the next movement harder. “Too much” makes it gentler. The session continues from where you were.

Careful by design

  • Pain stops the session. Mention pain, injury or dizziness and the movements are replaced with a calm stop, plus a pointer to proper help.
  • No overclaiming. Lull can’t see anyone. It never says someone exercised, only how long a timer ran.
  • No streaks, no guilt. No badges, no “you missed a day”. Breaks are meant to feel like breaks.
  • Accessible. Keyboard control, screen-reader announcements, reduced motion, and WCAG AA contrast in light and evening themes.

We can’t see your people. Neither can you.

Nothing leaves the device

No account and no tracking pixels. The activity log stays in the person’s browser, and they can switch it off or clear it.

Totals, never people

The team view shows company and team totals only. No names, no individual sessions, and nothing anyone typed.

Small groups stay hidden

Teams under 10 people are never shown. If only one team is that small, one more is hidden too, so nobody can work it out by subtraction.

A team view you can actually use

See whether people are trying it, whether they keep coming back, and when in the day they take breaks. That’s enough to judge a pilot without watching anyone.

  • People active each week, and how many are still using it after a month
  • Totals by team, for teams of 10 or more
  • When breaks happen, to time reminders sensibly
  • How people felt afterwards, from an optional one-tap question

Reminders where people already are

The hardest part is coming back. The next step we’re planning is a gentle reminder inside your team chat, at a time each person chooses, that opens a session in one tap.

  • Only for people who turn it on, at the time they pick
  • “Not today” means not today: no follow-up, no guilt
  • Messages are private to each person

This is a design concept. It isn’t built yet.

Questions

Is this medical advice?

No. Lull suggests gentle movement and stops when someone reports pain. It doesn’t diagnose or treat anything. A physiotherapist review of every movement is planned before any wider launch.

Do people need to install anything?

No. It runs in the browser. People can add it to their home screen or desktop if they like.

What does it cost?

Pilot pricing is per team and hasn’t been finalised. Ask us when you request a pilot.

Does it use AI?

By default it runs on fixed, tested logic with no AI model and no running cost. A live-model mode exists but stays off unless someone deliberately turns it on.

Which languages?

English for now. Hindi is on the roadmap.
