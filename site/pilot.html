Running a pilot

3 founding pilot slots

6 weeks. One team of 10–50. Indicative price: ₹60–120 per person per month. No annual contract. At ~30% participation you pay ~3.3x per actual participant; Lull's target (≥50% try it) means ~2x. The 50% try target is a goal, not a result.

What employees get

  • One 3-minute movement reset between calls, in English or Hinglish.
  • Five office-safe resets: neck & shoulders, back & hips, energy, calm, eyes & wrists.
  • Seated on a call or standing at the desk, switching without losing their place.
  • Safety stop: pain, dizziness or injury ends the reset and points to proper help.
  • Opt-in reminder on their own calendar or Slack after each session.

What HR gets

  • Team totals only: weekly active people, who is still using it, and what each team needs most.
  • Teams under 10 people are never shown.
  • A check-in at week 3 and a plain written report at week 6.
  • No individual data, no typed messages, no pain or safety data.

What you have to do

  1. Pick one team of 10–50 people. 15-minute kick-off. Share a link.
  2. Review team totals at week 3. Adjust anything that isn't working.
  3. At week 6, receive a written readout: what worked, what didn't, and whether to continue.

Who this is for: 50–500 person companies with desk-heavy teams. Teams of 10+.
Who this is not for: Companies wanting clinical mental-health support.

What happens after you click: 15-minute call → choose one team and agree targets → launch in a week.

FAQ

What if nobody uses it?

Lull doesn't rely on a launch email: after a session, each person can put their own weekday reminder in their calendar or Slack, and you'll see how many did. If usage still stalls, you'll know by week 3, not at renewal. The targets (at least half the team tries it, at least 1 in 3 comes back within a week) are agreed with you before we start, we check the team totals together at week 3 and adjust anything that isn't working, and if the targets aren't met, the week 6 report will say so plainly.

Can HR see what someone typed?

No. You see totals, never a person: no names, no individual sessions, nothing anyone typed. Teams under 10 people are never shown. Who reported pain is never shared with anyone, and people can switch off the activity log at any time.

Is this therapy or a mental-health service?

No. Lull offers three-minute desk resets you can do in work clothes, seated or standing, in the browser with nothing to install: for the neck and shoulders, the back and hips, a post-lunch slump, a busy mind, or tired eyes and wrists. It is not medical advice. If someone mentions pain, dizziness or injury, the reset stops and points them to proper help.

Won't breaks cost us work time?

Three minutes, at a time each person picks. In a review of 22 studies on breaks under 10 minutes, people felt more energetic and less tired, and the studies didn't find work suffered. Lull doesn't claim a productivity boost; it claims a short break people actually take.

We already have wellness through our insurance. Why this?

Keep it. Insurance and counselling benefits cover the serious moments. Lull covers the ordinary Tuesday afternoon: a stiff neck after back-to-back calls. It works alongside them, with nothing to switch.

Does it work for remote, field and shift teams?

Yes, the same way for everyone: a link on any phone or laptop, at a time each person chooses, including night shifts. Office-only perks like gym tie-ups miss these teams; Lull doesn't.

What happens in the pilot?

Week 0: pick one team of 10–50 people, a 15-minute kick-off, share a link. Weeks 1–3: people use it whenever they want. Week 3: a check-in on the team totals. Weeks 4–6: short interviews with up to 5 volunteers, then a plain written report: what worked, what didn't, and whether to continue.

Targets and stop criteria

Proposed kill criteria (clearly labelled):

  • Under 30% of the team tries a session by week 2.
  • Under 1 in 5 return for a second session within a week.
  • Under 25% set an opt-in reminder.
  • Any unresolved safety issue (pain report not stopped).
  • HR cannot name a decision they would make differently.

If any of the above occur, the pilot will be paused or ended. These are proposed criteria, not customer guarantees.
