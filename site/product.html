The problem

HR can't prove a wellbeing benefit is used; employees distrust tools that can see individuals. Without visible usage data, budgets shrink and founders override. Employees avoid tools that feel like surveillance.

What I tried / alternatives

  1. Do nothing — the status quo; no proof, no risk, no cost, but no benefit either.
  2. Gym benefit — on-site or discounted gym membership. High usage among the already-active; low reach for desk-heavy teams.
  3. EAP / counselling — employee assistance programme. Reactive, focused on crisis; stigma prevents uptake.
  4. Generic wellbeing app — another app to download, another login. Retention collapses: median 3.3% at 30 days (JMIR 2019, 93 apps).
  5. Calendar reminder / YouTube stretch — free, but no tracking, no community, no data for HR.
  6. Lull — between calls, 3 minutes, no install, no account, manager ritual. The wedge.

The wedge

Between calls, 3 minutes, no install, no account, manager ritual. Lull fits in the gap left by bigger, scarier tools.

Three hard decisions

Privacy
Allow-listed anonymous counts only: session-started/finished, feeling, broad need category, reminder set, came-back, opened-from-reminder, team-reset-joined. Teams under 10 hidden, and a second team hidden if subtraction could reveal one. No typed text leaves the device.
Safety
Fixed rules above the model. The model can add caution, never remove it. Hinglish pain words trigger the same stop as English. eval evidence: word list alone missed 10 of 14 body concerns; list plus Claude missed none.
Distribution
Calendar/Slack reminders and team resets instead of another app. People do what their team does; this is often what makes it stick.

What I built

  • App with real engine (need matching, safety rules, five desk resets), no AI by default.
  • Site with HR/TA decision path, PM case study, data handbook, pilot pack.
  • Tests: privacy contract, Hinglish safety, core flows (measured output, not hand-maintained numbers).
  • Eval: 44 realistic messages, keyword layer vs Claude on safety.
  • Pilot kit: launch kit, sample report, target picker, week-6 readout template.

How I measure it

Adoption, return within a week, reminder opt-in, team reach, cost per participating employee, safety stops, zero data incidents. No health/productivity/ROI claims. The 64-person company is synthetic; there are no customer results yet.

What would make me stop

Proposed kill criteria (clearly labelled):

  • Under 30% try by week 2.
  • Under 1 in 5 return within a week.
  • Under 25% set a reminder.
  • Any unresolved safety issue.
  • HR cannot name a decision they would make differently.

These are proposed criteria, not customer guarantees.

What I got wrong

  • Workflow companion rebuild — over-engineered, wrong persona onboarding.
  • Name / persona onboarding cut — added friction, not reduced.
  • Safety floor added — after eval showed keyword list missed body concerns.

Open questions and what I'd do next

  • Can Hinglish understanding be improved without Claude?
  • What other between-calls moments are underserved?
  • How can HR teams be involved in co-design without extra workload?
